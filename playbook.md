## Where the room is
An edit removes an obstacle that hides an ability; it does not add one. It can at most make the model behave the
best way its weights already can, so measure that ceiling first, without editing: the failure kinds of every task
type (`acc when finished` against `acc`; a failure's share differs a lot between task types, so math alone can miss
an obstacle elsewhere), pass@k against accuracy (`run --n 4`), and prompt or sampling overrides (`run <case>
base@hint=<name> <set>`). Status of the routes below on Qwen3.5-0.8B thinking:
- **Spent** - reasoning loops (removed by the loop edit) and answers cut at the token limit: stacked on several
  published edits the model is DEV 68-76% right, 3-7% cut, 72-78% right when finished; the cuts are almost all math
  (7-20% of math answers), and closers at that cut share gain nothing ("Closing thinking that runs long").
- **Open** - most misses are now finished wrong answers. Fitting on the model's own samples is the route with
  confirmed HOLDOUT gains; it is bounded by pass@k, not by a failure share, so measure pass@k (and, for answer
  fits, the mixed share of `branch`) on the start first. Rounds on the answer exhaust it (see "Fitting on the
  model's own samples"): the room left is inside the thinking, where traces are decided early (`branch --at`) and
  where a way of working can be primed and distilled (`@prime=`). Unsettled: how much of the IF gain is the branch
  pairs and how much the KL tether, and how to keep math from dropping while fitting other sources (`--anchor`).
- **Dead ends** - "Tried and not confirmed" below; do not repeat them without a new idea.

Models screened (`cases/screening`): loops dominate only in small Qwen3.5 models in thinking mode (0.8B strongly, 2B
moderately); Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B, Qwen3.5-4B, R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, SmolLM3-3B and Phi-4-mini-reasoning show no removable
failure on GSM8K/MATH train (the post's Gemma 3 270M follow-up is the same negative case). On GSM8K, IFEval and
MMLU-Pro: OpenReasoning-Nemotron-1.5B loops (13% of GSM8K answers); Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507, LFM2.5-1.2B-Thinking,
Granite-3.3-2B, Phi-4-mini, OLMo-2-1B, SmolLM2-1.7B and Qwen2.5-1.5B show only reasoning errors and unboxed answers.
Qwen3.5-2B thinking after the loop edit: no hint or temperature helps, finished math answers are ~99% right and misses
are hard problems cut at 32k (long reasoning, not loops); Qwen3.5-4B thinking is near saturated (AMC 95.6, GSM8K
96.0); LFM2.5-1.2B-Thinking never truncates.

## Measuring and choosing
- Always run a control with the same size of change: a norm-matched random orthogonal direction, or the same
  fitted values on random units. It separates a specific effect from a generic perturbation; it is not always null
  (random heads -3.1, a shuffled fitted twin -12.9 on one source, a random direction at high dose -3.5), so
  measure it on DEV.
- **Selection noise.** At 100-250 problems x 1-2 samples the paired SE is 2-3 points, so the best of many variants
  on one DEV is inflated (DEV +7.8 / +6.5 / +7.0 confirmed at -0.6 / +3.0 / -3.7; neighbouring settings of one edit
  spread from -1.6 to +6.3). Seeds of one fit spread about as much as the effect (12 seed x dose candidates on one
  DEV: -6.1 to +2.5; 4 seeds on 1200 answers: -0.7 to +1.0 and -2.5 to +0.7), and two DEV halves of 160 problems
  disagree (+5.0 ± 2.5 vs +0.6 ± 2.2; +1.2 vs -1.9 ± 1.9). Trust gaps of about 2 SE, add samples before choosing,
  and confirm once on a set no choice has seen (a subset of DEV is not one). A pooled gain can hide a loss on one
  task type: at ~32 problems per source the per-source SE is 5-8 points, so a per-source check needs more problems
  per source, and an accept/reject floor per source must scale with its SE.
- Compare edits only paired; unpaired passes of 128 answers are ~4 points noisy.
- Gate a delivery on harm, not on a gain: when the effect is about one SE, "deliver only if every check is up" is a
  coin flip per check and usually delivers nothing; hold back an edit that loses beyond ~2 SE on some source.
- Before writing a decoding change into the weights (a logit bias, a forced close), measure it at decoding
  (`base@penalize=...`, `base@hint=...`): an edit that applies it everywhere should not beat it, so a larger gain is
  likely noise; only a gate that applies it where it helps can do better.
- A teacher-forced answer start ("The correct answer is: \boxed{") is a fast screen (minutes) but overstates the
  effect for models that reason before answering: check the failure's share in real generation first.

## Directions and the components that write them
- The loop edit: for each chosen layer, `W_down -= alpha * u (u^T W_down)[:, top-k]`, with `u` the residual
  direction separating loop continuations from normal reasoning (mean difference over matched pairs from the model's
  own correct traces) and the top-k MLP neurons writing most along `u`. Layers 15 and 19 of 24, k = 64, alpha = 6
  for both Qwen3.5 sizes; the 0.8B case reproduces the post's result independently.
- Contrast pairs (same prefix, normal vs failure continuation) give a direction stable across layers (split-half
  cosine > 0.9); the writer analysis localizes the edit, and within a model family the whole recipe transfers.
- **Selectivity on held-out pairs is a cheap pre-screen**: good edits lower the failure continuation's log-prob
  (~0.1 nats/token) and leave the normal one nearly unchanged (< 0.02); a drop of the normal log-prob predicts a
  loss in generation. It is not sufficient: on the loop-edited 0.8B, very selective loop edits still lost 6-10
  points in generation.
- Ablating a direction from the last layer's MLP hurts even when it is the largest writer: it is entangled with
  next-token prediction (a single gated unit there can still write a token-level change, see the "Wait" penalty).
- Dose is the main knob; larger models saturate at lower alpha. Adding layers does not help once the failure share
  is small.
- Removing loops is not math-specific: the edits, selected on math practice only, lift IFEval, MMLU-Pro and BBH,
  where loops cost even more (2B: 41% of MMLU-Pro and 48% of BBH answers loop to the token limit; 0.8B: 91% cut on
  MMLU-Pro). It does not force early stopping: correct answers get longer, because harder problems now finish.

## Fitting on the model's own samples
When the model solves a problem in some samples but not in others (pass@k well above accuracy) and no single
direction explains the failures, fitting on its own practice samples (its finished, correct answers, or its right
against its wrong ones) moves it toward its better samples (`wsp.fit`; `wsp.case branch` for answer branches).
- Loop-ablated Qwen3.5-0.8B thinking: per-unit MLP and per-head gains, three fits on 80% of ~1000 own correct
  answers averaged in log space, dose 1.25: practice HOLDOUT +7.2 ± 1.9 (cut share 25% -> 14%), a rehearsal from
  scratch +6.8 ± 1.9; the gain is mostly answers that now finish.
- Another parameterisation on the same kind of data (gradient-refit output columns of ~650 MLP units, ~600 own
  correct answers) gave +6.4 ± 1.8 on its holdout; keeping only short answers did not matter (DEV +13.3 without the
  length filter vs +8.2 / +14.1 with it).
- **Separate the answer from the thinking.** Resampling only the answer after the model's own fixed thinking shows
  whether a failure is made in the answer: on IF practice (ifsynth, tuluif) 17-20% of prompts are mixed, some
  branches keeping a constraint the thinking noted and others breaking it. IF failures are scattered over constraint
  types, so a unit per constraint helps only its own (no_comma +20.4 ± 5.2, highlighted sections +16.8 ± 5.8, other
  types -1.6 ± 2.0), while a preference fit on the branches reaches them all: DPO + NLL on the passing branch,
  branch tokens only, gains of ~650 MLP units, four seeds averaged at dose 0.35, HOLDOUT +7.5 ± 2.1 (tuluif +22.5,
  ifsynth +10.0, math -1.2); in another run at dose 0.5, HOLDOUT ifsynth +1.8 ± 1.8, mmlupro +4.7 ± 1.9, math -2.1
  ± 1.7. In a related fit the KL tether alone moved tuluif as much as the pairs (+3.7 vs +3.5 ± 1.4).
- **Early decisions inside the thinking.** A passing and a failing trace of one problem, each cut at 10/25/50% of
  its thinking and continued: the passing trace's starts end right 48/55/62% of the time, the failing one's
  38/34/30% (math levels 4-5), so most traces are decided well before they end; a failing trace restarted at
  25/50/75% ends right 31/24/20% against 50% from scratch. Pairs of continuations of one start (`branch --at`,
  DPO + NLL on the first 2048 tokens after it: `--window`, gains of MLP units and heads in layers 4-21, seeds
  averaged): math practice +2.8 ± 0.6 at dose 1.0 (1200 problems x 4), +0.8 to +1.0 at 0.5; puzzles, IF and
  mmlupro within noise.
- **Distilling a primed way of working.** Opening the thinking with one sentence in the model's own voice
  (`data.PRIMES` "brief", "plan") lifts math practice +3.0 to +4.6 when forced, but forced everywhere it costs
  puzzles (89 -> 77) and IF (-5). Fitting the right primed answers' first 160 tokens after the plain question
  (`run base@prime=brief`, then `fit --objective sft --window 160 --params units --units 64-128`, other sources
  held with `--anchor`) kept the gain where it helps: math +2.4 and +3.5 ± 1.1 in rehearsals, +0.9 ± 1.1 on a
  stacked start, puzzles -0.7 to -1.3. Distilling the right answers without the sentence lost (-1.3, -2.2), and so
  did a primed procedure for IF (-5.3) or word problems (-1.2).
- **Rounds on the answer run dry.** After a few IF answer rounds the mixed share of answer branches is 3-5%, and of
  1,589 remaining IF failures 1,133 had no passing branch (the failure is made in the thinking); a further IF round
  on a start that carries one gained nothing (held-out requests -1.3 ± 1.0). Holding the other sources (KL to the
  start's own answers over the whole vocabulary, `--anchor`) cut an IF round's puzzle cost by about two thirds
  (-1.4 -> -0.4 to -0.5 ± 0.5) for about half of its IF gain (+16.0 -> +7.6 to +9.7).
- **Singular-value scales** (`--params svd`: the top 48 of the residual writers in layers 4-21, each an exact
  `dir_ablate` op) fitted by policy gradients stayed near noise on practice: RLOO credited at answer tokens plus a teacher term (JSD to the model shown the
  verified answer) DEV +1.0 ± 1.8, fresh IF practice +1.4 to +3.7 over three candidates; credited at high-entropy
  thinking tokens and applied reversed (against concluding sooner), seed screens within noise and more cut math
  answers, and with a late closer one HOLDOUT +3.5 ± 1.8 from parts that were each within noise.

## Closing thinking that runs long
A closer gains, net, the cut answers it turns right minus the right answers it breaks, so it pays roughly in
proportion to the start's cut share. Left alone, answers still thinking at 28k of 32k tokens end right 3% (n = 100);
closed, 6-38% of the dead-end runs end right (five runs), while short right answers lose 2-9 points where the gate
fires early.
- The conclude direction (contrast `conclude`: keep verifying vs close, once the result is stated) from attention
  outputs (o_proj of layers 11-23, alpha 0.75-1): HOLDOUT +8.4 ± 1.5 and +3.4 / +3.9 ± 1.5 on starts cutting 20-25%
  (random directions +0.8, -1.4); +4.1 ± 1.4 with the loop direction on o_proj 11, 23 at 13.5% cut (+2.4 ± 1.5 at
  3%, the cut unchanged); +0.7 ± 1.2 and -0.6 ± 1.2 on the strongest starts (5-7% cut). A higher alpha won on DEV
  and confirmed lower (+6.0 vs +8.4). From MLP writers it is not selective (it lowers the closing continuation too):
  HOLDOUT +0.8 ± 2.0.
- Where half the answers were cut (the loop-edited 0.8B) closing near the token limit paid: a unit reading the
  length (L10, rarely firing below 8k tokens) plus units opening the answer, math HOLDOUT +9.8 (cut 51% -> 4%), no
  source worse; a gated "Wait" penalty (one last-layer unit, an LDA read with instruction prompts as negatives)
  HOLDOUT +4.6 ± 1.5 (the holdout also entered its choice), while ungated or stronger it leaks into instruction
  following (-3.1). The same kind of unit on one later start: math HOLDOUT +4.4 ± 2.0 in one run, about 0 and +1 on
  DEV in two others.
- Closers on starts cutting 5-18% removed nearly every cut without a gain beyond noise (+1.0 ± 1.7, -0.8 ± 1.2;
  HOLDOUT +0.3 ± 2.1, -1.6 ± 2.1).
- Forcing `\boxed{` after a late close makes the late-closed answers box first (0 -> 0.63 / 0.85) without making
  more of them right (-1.0 ± 1.8, -2.1 ± 1.6 against the closer alone).
- Closing early costs instruction following: units that close the thinking on a settled answer, or steer toward
  "execute", gained 1-1.5 SE on math and lost 3-12 points of IF, growing with the dose, even when built silent on
  instruction prompts.

## Tried and not confirmed (do not repeat without a new idea)
Paired gains on DEV128 / HOLDOUT256 after the loop edit (2B unless noted).
- **Effective temperature and structural loop breakers on the 0.8B thinking model**: final-norm x0.7-0.8 (T ~0.75;
  norms are not editable anyway) +0.4 / +1.4 on DEV128 x4: loops become long non-repeating reasoning, still cut.
  Gated-DeltaNet decay, key-input and MLP "repeat vs first occurrence" directions: no selective effect on real loops.
  A loop-onset direction from the loop-edited model's own traces, ablated from linear-attention outputs, trades
  verbatim loops for long deliberation (cut by loops 9 -> 1, other cuts 42 -> 58; HOLDOUT accuracy unchanged).
- **Pulling toward the pre-trained checkpoint or away from doubt** (2B thinking after the loop edit): merging with
  its Base checkpoint hurts both ways (lam -0.1: MATH500 -8.0). SAE features of doubt and backtracking (Qwen-Scope)
  are more active early in traces that end cut, but removing them costs MATH500 4.5-7.5 points: symptoms of hard
  problems, not habits.
- **Opening preference** (DPO on right vs wrong openings of the same problem, gsm8k / mmlupro pairs, with a KL or
  likelihood tether, 0.8B): small and mixed; HOLDOUT gsm8k +2.3 ± 1.1, ifsynth +2.7 ± 1.6 and +7.0 ± 3.9, math -0.2
  and -3.1 (no math pairs).
- **Damping copy heads against loops** (heads with the highest copy / normal ratio, `scale_cols` on their o_proj
  block, loop-edited 0.8B): the top 7 lose at every factor (-0.8 to -16 DEV); the best of ~25 variants (+2.7 ± 2.5)
  has neighbours from -2.3 to +0.4: noise.
- **Correct vs wrong reasoning** (contrast `correct`, Qwen3-0.6B, pass@8 96% vs pass@1 76%): split-half stability of
  the direction is negative (-0.15 to -0.39) and the separation < 0.55; "being right" is not one direction.
- **Second-guessing** (contrast `secondguess`: a wrong answer that had stated the right one, Qwen3-0.6B, 5% of
  samples): the direction is very stable (0.95) and separated, yet the edit is null at alpha 3 (-0.7 ± 1.4, random
  +1.1 ± 1.2) and harmful at alpha 6 (-5.8 ± 1.8, n = 8 per problem). The share is only 2-2.4% in Qwen3-1.7B,
  R1-Distill-1.5B and SmolLM3-3B. Even the prompt "trust a careful result" does nothing (-0.5 ± 1.3).
- **Prompt hints as the ceiling for baking a prompt into weights**: "keep reasoning concise" on the loop-fixed 2B
  gives +1.8 ± 1.0 (cut 18.6% -> 16.9%); too small to be worth writing into the weights.
- **Whole-model transforms on loop-free Qwen3** (0.6B, DEV128 x 8 samples): extrapolating post-training,
  `W + a (W - W_pretrained)`, a = 0.2 +0.1 ± 1.6 (1.7B -1.1 ± 0.9), a = -0.1 +0.3 ± 1.3, a = 0.5 runs away into long
  outputs; attention temperature (all q_norm x 1.1 / 1.25) +0.4 / +0.5; sampling temperature 0.4 -0.6; a logit
  penalty on "Wait"/"Alternatively" (thought switching) -1.4 ± 1.2 (R1-1.5B -0.6 ± 0.9). Their errors are reasoning
  errors; nothing global moves them (gated, where answers are often cut, see "Closing thinking that runs long").
- **Too-short reasoning** (ThinkEdit's failure): absent here, short traces are the most accurate ones (0.6B 95%,
  R1-1.5B 100% vs 54% / 76% for traces over 6k characters).
- **Non-thinking mode** (0.8B): wrong answers are genuine reasoning errors, no removable failure. Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B
  already reason before answering; "reason step by step" moves MMLU-Pro / BBH by -3 to +3 (300 items), so there is
  no hidden "answers too fast" gap. Their "no answer" share (0.6B: 23-34%) is unboxed answers, a grader matter.
- **Written workings and rule sentences for single puzzle families** (a program writes each family's working, or a
  sentence states its rule, fitted into rewritten units): large on the families they are written for (+18 to +22)
  and nothing elsewhere (other families +0.0 ± 0.9, word problems +0.0, IF -2.3); they teach a family, not reasoning.
- **Group credit from restarts and RLOO on own math answers**: GRPO on continuations of a wrong trace alone -0.2 ±
  0.7; RLOO on whole math samples -0.5 to -2.2.
- **Decoding instead of weights**: repetition penalty 1.1 on the unedited 2B gives +11.7 ± 3.8 on DEV, the weight
  edit +17.2 ± 3.7; the edit is better and needs no change to the sampler, but most of the loop gain is reachable
  by decoding.

## Engineering
- One job per GPU (tools/queue.sh starts a worker per GPU, so N GPUs run N experiments at once). One 24 GB GPU: vLLM
  startup ~4 min; a looping 0.8B at 32k tokens ~3,000 tok/s (128 problems ~15-30 min, MATH-500 ~1.5 h), an edited,
  rarely looping one 3,600-4,400 tok/s per RTX 4090. Probes take minutes.
