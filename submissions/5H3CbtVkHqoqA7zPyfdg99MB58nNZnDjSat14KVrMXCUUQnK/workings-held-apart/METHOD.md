# Workings held apart: the model learns to write a short working for puzzles of a fixed form, and hold examples keep every other task where it was; on top of a round of answer branching on new requests

This entry starts from the champion (`entry.json`: `"parent": "champion"`). It derives two parts in the run and
delivers them as one edit:

- **R, a round of answer branching on new requests.** The method and the scripts of `0009550031-0005`
  (`derive/rollouts.py`, `derive/branch.py`, `derive/train_sparse.py`, `derive/avg_sparse.py`,
  `derive/dose_sparse.py`): keep an answer's thinking, sample the answer again, and fit a preference between a
  passing and a failing answer written after the same thinking. The requests are recombined from the practice
  sets' own material (`derive/make_requests.py`; a second round on recombined requests was first published by
  `0009559255-0006`), every instruction type asked often, eight new answers after every thinking. The fits
  carry hold examples too (the start's own answers to word problems, math and multiple choice), and with them the
  round is delivered at the dose 0.7 where the published rounds stop at 0.35.
- **P, a puzzle layer.** For puzzles whose every instance has the same form (a schedule to search, a chain of
  swaps to follow, an order to rebuild) a program writes the working such a puzzle needs and nothing more
  (`derive/workings.py`), and the model is fitted to write it itself. The fit sits on the model that carries R, on
  other neurons, and is **held in place everywhere else**: the model's own answers to requests, word problems,
  math, multiple choice and the puzzle kinds that come in many forms enter the fit as **hold examples**, scored by
  how far the edited model's next-token distributions have moved from the starting model's along that answer.
- **F = R and P in one edit**, every changed row written as one direction of int8 values (`derive/pack.py`): two
  layers of 650 neurons in about 4.0 MB.

Where it comes from, plainly. The branching method and its code are the published work of the author of `0009550031-0005`. The trainer is that entry's `train_sparse.py` with one addition, the hold term (about 60 lines:
a switch that turns the trained changes off for a reference pass, the divergence of the two passes, the example
type). New here:

- **what the start does on fixed-form puzzles** (section 1): it thinks for 8,000 to 16,000 tokens, re-reads and
  re-checks, and loses the thread: a third of the schedules right, half of the adjective orders;
- **a working written by a program is enough.** Nothing is solved for the model: the working copies the facts of
  the puzzle into a fixed layout, applies the one rule of the kind line by line and states the conclusion. Fitted
  on 300 of them per kind, the model writes the layout itself on new puzzles in 200 to 900 tokens and is right
  far more often (section 1);
- **why such a fit cannot be used as it is.** Learned freely, the short style spreads to everything: word
  problems fell from 76% to 53%. With the usual likelihood anchors the collapse is gone but every other task
  still reads one to three points lower and the request layer loses up to half of its gain;
- **hold examples.** An anchor scores the tokens the model happened to sample, averaged over the answer: a change
  at five decision points of a 5,000-token answer is invisible in it. A hold example compares the whole next-token
  distributions of the starting model and the edited model at every position of the answer and SUMS the
  divergence over the answer. It is exactly zero, with zero gradient, while the fit does nothing there, so it adds
  no noise at any weight. With it the divergence on other tasks falls by a factor of ten to twenty, the answers
  keep their length, and the request layer keeps its whole gain (section 1);
- **the same hold examples in the request fits.** The published rounds stop at the dose 0.35 because more costs
  multiple choice and math and buys nothing. Held on the start's own answers to those tasks, the fitted change
  can be delivered at twice the dose: more requests followed, and the price on multiple choice gone (section 1);
- **which puzzles get a working.** Only kinds whose every instance has one form. Kinds that come in many forms
  (dates, tables, coloured objects, arguments, word sorting) get none: a layout forced on a form it does not fit
  does harm. They are hold examples too;
- **two full layers in one edit** (section 1, "packing").

Every number below is the author's local measurement on practice data and on puzzles generated by
`derive/puzzles.py`, for orientation. No arrays are shipped: R, P and F are derived in the run from practice
data, generated puzzles and the starting model's own generations.

## 1. What the start loses, and what the two layers do

Measured on two related starts: the champion's model `0009554110-0010` (marked C) and the published
`0009554615-0007` (marked D), four answers per problem unless said, paired by problem, standard errors after "+-".

**Fixed-form puzzles (generated, 21 per kind kept apart from every fit; start D).**

| kind | start: right | tokens per answer | with F: right | tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| schedules (when was the person free) | 36% | 10,900 | 100% | 310 |
| adjective order | 52% | 8,800 | 100% | 250 |
| shapes from a drawing path | 61% | 9,200 | 95% | 260 |
| swaps among three / five / seven people | 91 / 94 / 88% | 7,200 / 8,100 / 10,200 | 100 / 98 / 95% | 180 / 290 / 470 |
| ordering three / five / seven things | 83 / 80 / 76% | 5,700 / 12,400 / 16,100 | 94 / 96 / 61% | 260 / 380 / 860 |
| directions on a grid | 79% | 7,500 | 87% | 250 |
| truth values, arithmetic in brackets, who lies, counting | 89 / 94 / 96 / 80% | 3,200 to 5,400 | 93 / 100 / 100 / 81% | 170 to 500 |
| all 19 kinds (the five held kinds included) | 79.1% | 7,600 | 89.9% (+10.8 +- 1.6) | 1,800 |

On a second set of 1,140 puzzles: +12.0 +- 0.9. The five kinds without a working stay where they were (between
-1.8 and +2.4, each +- 3 to 6). Seven-thing orderings are the one worked kind that reads lower on the small set
(61% against 76%; +6.0 on the large one): the working is kept for them.

**The same puzzle layer on the champion's model (C)**, fitted on top of a request layer: 88.7% of the kept puzzles
(schedules 99%, adjective order 99%, swaps 100 / 98 / 100%).

**What holds the fit in place.** The divergence between the starting model and the edited model, summed over one
of the starting model's own answers (nats per answer; `train_sparse.py` prints it for the held-out hold examples):

| fit of the puzzle layer | word problems | math | multiple choice | requests | word problems right (start 75.9%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no control | 121 | 127 | 212 | 126 to 144 | 53.2% |
| likelihood anchors (2,200 of the model's own answers) | 118 | 123 | 213 | 86 to 89 | 74.6% (-1.8 +- 0.8) |
| hold examples (1,400), weight 0.03 | 7 | 10 | 14 | 11 to 13 | 75.7% (-0.25 +- 0.5, three draws) |

For comparison, the request layer R alone moves an answer of another task by 6 to 9 nats.

**The request layer under the puzzle layer (start D; requests whose tasks no fit saw).**

| | R alone | R + puzzle layer with anchors | R + puzzle layer with hold examples (F) |
|---|---|---|---|
| recombined requests, 402 | +12.6 +- 1.3 | +9.0 | +12.4 +- 1.3 |
| a larger set of 1,200 | +12.0 +- 0.8 | +5.0 | +11.7 +- 0.8 |
| shipped one-instruction requests, 412 | +3.7 +- 0.7 | +1.6 | +4.1 +- 0.8 |

The hold examples that matter here are the request layer's OWN answers to requests: the fit of P is held on what
R does, not on what the start did.

**The request layer with and without hold examples (start D; the same pairs, neurons protected and seeds).**

| fit of the request layer | dose | recombined requests, 402 | one-instruction requests, 412 | multiple choice | divergence on word problems / math / multiple choice (nats per answer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no hold examples (the published recipe) | 0.35 | +12.6 +- 1.3 | +3.7 +- 0.7 | -1.1 +- 0.65 (three draws) | 5.6 / 5.8 / 9.3 |
| hold examples (500) | 0.35 | +10.9 +- 1.2 | +4.9 +- 0.8 | -0.9 +- 0.9 | 1.9 / 2.3 / 3.5 |
| hold examples | 0.7 | +16.8 +- 1.5 | | 0.0 +- 0.9 | 2.9 / 3.8 / 5.5 |
| hold examples | 1.0 | +19.0 +- 1.6 | | +0.3 +- 1.0 | |

At the same dose the held fit gains a little less (the hold is not free); the dose it allows pays for that
several times over. 0.7 is the delivered dose: the gain at 1.0 is larger still, and so is the share of answers
that end without a readable answer (0.2% -> 1.1%).

**What the weight and the amount of data do.** The divergence left on other tasks is the same from weight 0.01 to
0.2, while a weight above 0.03 costs the puzzles (the same data: +7.2 at 0.01, +4.0 at 0.05). Too few workings
show as a layout that is written but slips inside (with 76 per kind: directions 77%, shapes 76%, seven-people swaps
86%; with 300 per kind 87 to 100%). Hence 300 per kind and weight 0.03.

**Other tasks under the two layers.** With a request layer fitted without hold examples (start D): math
+0.2 +- 1.2 on each of two sets of 200 problems; multiple choice -1.1 +- 0.9, the same as that request layer alone:
the price the hold examples in the request fits take away (table below). On the champion's model (C), the two
layers as this entry delivers them (the request layer at 0.7, the puzzle layer on top): word problems -0.1 +- 0.5
and multiple choice -0.3 +- 0.6 (three draws each), math -3.3 +- 1.3 and 0.0 +- 1.3 on two sets of 200 problems
(the request layer alone: -1.8 +- 1.3 and -0.3 +- 1.2). The puzzle fit holds 450 of the model's own multiple-choice
answers: with 150 the same build read 1.1 +- 0.8 lower on multiple choice, 2.1 +- 1.2 lower on the recombined
requests and 1.2 +- 0.8 lower on the one-instruction requests (paired), and 0.8 +- 0.6 higher on word problems.

**Packing.** A whole-neuron layer stored as float16 rows is 4.0 MB for 650 neurons, so two layers do not fit.
`dir_ablate` normalises its direction when it is loaded, so a changed row needs only the DIRECTION of its change,
one int8 per value, and the size of the step as the op's `alpha` (`derive/pack.py`): 1,025 bytes per row. Both
layers: 3,900 ops, 4,003,360 bytes of the 4,194,304 allowed; the packed change differs from the wanted one by
0.8% of its length, and the packed model reads like the unpacked one (puzzles +10.7 against +10.8, recombined
requests +12.8 against +12.4).

**Stop rules.** Fewer than 400 pairs for R: deliver nothing new for R and say so (the published round had 391).
The puzzle fit's own readout "hold rows: divergence per answer" above 30 nats at the end, or F below 78% on
the kept puzzles (step D): deliver R alone.

- Use only `practice:` data, the puzzles `derive/puzzles.py` generates and the models' own generations. Never load

## 3. Steps

All GPU work is one detached chain: one step after the other on the four GPUs, each with its own log, and a
finished step is never run twice. Start it once, look at it every 15 minutes, and do by hand only what needs
judgement: the readings, the uploads, the report.

```
nohup python derive/chain.py run > chain.out 2>&1 &
python derive/chain.py status        # done / RUNNING / waiting per step, the last lines of the running step's log
```

| step (log in `/workspace/work/rq/logs/`) | what it does | done after about |
|---|---|---|
| `requests` | the recombined requests: about 4,300 for the fit on tasks outside dev and holdout, about 540 held-out ones on the tasks of dev and about 900 on those of the holdout | 2 min |
| `answers` | the model's own answers to the training requests (x 2, 10,240-token limit) and their table | 30 min |
| `branch` | for both answers of each request: the thinking kept, eight new answers sampled after it and graded; the pairs | 55 min |
| `single` | the same for the shipped one-instruction `tuluif` requests outside dev and holdout (four answers each) | 1 h 10 min |
| `anchors` | the model's own answers to 650 other practice problems (200 word problems, 150 math, 300 multiple choice): hold examples of the request fits, and the neurons they lean on most stay out of R | 1 h 20 min |
| `fit1`, `fit23`, `fit4` | four preference fits of one neuron set (the first chooses the neurons) | 2 h 45 min |
| `average` | their average at the dose 0.7 (`R`); the model that carries R built | 2 h 45 min |
| `puzzles` | generated puzzles: 300 per fixed-form kind to work on, 150 per many-form kind to hold, 20 per kind kept for the reading; the workings | 2 h 46 min |
| `holdans` | the model that carries R answers once: 450 requests, 300 word problems, 250 math, 550 multiple choice, the 750 puzzles to hold | 3 h 05 min |
| `fitp` | the puzzle fit on that model: workings + hold examples, R's neurons left alone, 20,000 protected neurons | 3 h 45 min |
| `pack` | R and P in one edit (`F`); its model built | 3 h 50 min |
| `check` | F on the 380 kept puzzles, two answers each; the table per kind | 3 h 55 min |

Times are estimates for four RTX 4090s from our own runs on faster cards (our times x 1.8). The run ends with
`axonry-done` right after the final upload (step D): nothing that runs after it changes what is judged.

- If `status` shows a step as FAILED in `rq/chain.log`, read the log it names, fix the cause, and start the chain
  again with the same command: finished steps are skipped.
- A generation job can hang when its engine shuts down (its log ends with the engine's shutdown lines, its GPU
  shows memory in use at 0% utilisation). Stop that job by its PID and start the chain again.
- Do not start other GPU jobs while the chain runs: it assumes the four GPUs are its own.

**Step A: the requests and the pairs (after about 1 h 10 min)**

- `logs/requests.log` prints the counts per kind and per number of instructions. `logs/answers.log` ends with the
  table of the start on the training requests. Write it in NOTES.
- `logs/branch.log` and `logs/single.log` print how often the outcome after the same thinking is mixed, and the
  number of pairs (ours on start D: about 1,900 + 290 pairs). Read three pairs of `/workspace/work/rq/pairs.jsonl`:
  `chosen` and `rejected` follow the same `prefix` and differ in whether an instruction holds.
- The stop rule of section 1: fewer than 400 pairs in all. Then stop the chain (`python derive/chain.py stop`) and
  say so in the report.

**Step B: R and the first upload (after about 2 h 45 min: `average` done)**

- `logs/anchors.log` ends with the counts of the request fits' data: the pairs and the hold examples per source.
  `logs/fit1.log` prints the protected neurons, the chosen neurons per layer and the payload. Each fit prints its
  training loss with the largest relative change of a row so far and the divergence on its hold examples, and at
  the end its validation loss (ours: the divergence on held-out hold examples stays near 10 nats per answer).
- A single fit is unreliable (the published finding). Only the average is used, and only at the dose 0.7.
- Upload `R` now: it is the edit that stays if the puzzle layer does not pass its checks.

  ```
  python -m wsp.upload rq R --task task.json --dry-run --report /workspace/work/cases/rq/NOTES.md
  axonry-check $WSP_WORK/deliver/rq__R/edit --task task.json
  python -m wsp.upload rq R --task task.json --report /workspace/work/REPORT.md
  ```

  Write NOTES.md and REPORT.md before these commands (what is known so far): both need their report file to exist.

**Step C: the puzzle layer, F and the second upload (after about 3 h 50 min: `pack` done)**

- `logs/puzzles.log`: puzzles per kind, how many were dropped, workings per kind (a puzzle whose working does not
  reproduce its known answer is skipped: expect none or a handful). Read two workings of
  `/workspace/work/rq/rows_work.jsonl`: the facts of the puzzle in a fixed layout, the rule applied line by line,
  the conclusion.
- `logs/holdans.log`: the answers of the model with R. `logs/fitp.log` begins with the counts of the fit's data
  (workings, hold examples per source) and prints during the fit, next to the loss of the workings,
  `hold rows: divergence per answer N nats` for the held-out hold examples. Ours: the loss of the workings from 1.5
  to below 0.01, the divergence rising to 30 to 60 in the first fifth of the fit and falling to about 10 at the end.
- `logs/pack.log`: the payload (ours 4,003,360 bytes) and how far the packed change is from the wanted one (ours
  0.8%).
- Checks before F replaces R. (1) The divergence printed at the end of `fitp` is at most 30 nats. (2) The payload is
  under the limit. If one fails: keep R as the delivered edit and say so in the report.
- Upload F with the three commands of step B (`F` in place of `R`).

**Step D: the kept puzzles, the report and the end (after about 3 h 55 min)**

- `logs/check.log` is the table of the 380 kept puzzles per kind for F: the share solved and the tokens per
  answer. Ours: all kinds together 86 to 90% (the start alone: 66%); the fourteen worked kinds 93% with answers of
  150 to 1,100 tokens; the five held kinds near the start, with answers as long as the start's.
- Keep F unless the line ALL is below 78%: then the puzzle layer did not take. Upload R again as the final edit
  (step B's commands) and say so in the report.
- REPORT.md: the tables of step A; the neurons per layer and the payloads of R and F; the data of the puzzle fit
  and its divergence readout; the kept puzzles per kind; what was uploaded and when; what did not work.
- Upload the final report with the edit (step B's commands once more), see that the upload is complete, and run
  `axonry-done`.

**If the chain runs late.** The uploads of steps B and C are what counts. With less than 30 minutes left and
`check` not done: stop the chain (`python derive/chain.py stop`, then stop the running generation job by PID), write
what was read so far into REPORT.md, upload it with the edit, and run `axonry-done`. No upload with fewer than 10
minutes left.

**The longer readings.** `READ=full python derive/chain.py run` reads, in place of `check`, the case's dev and
holdout sets and the held-out requests for the start, R and F (steps `phase1`, `phase2`, `report`; about an hour and
a half more). They are not part of this entry's run.

## 4. What to expect

- Read in the run: the kept puzzles. The worked kinds from about 64% to about 93%, with answers of a few hundred
  tokens in place of several thousand; the five held kinds near the start (20 puzzles per kind, two answers:
  standard errors of 5 to 10 points on one kind).
- Not read in the run (ours on the champion's model, with `READ=full`): the held-out requests up by 10 to 13
  points with R and with F alike (540 and 909 requests, two answers each); word problems, math and multiple choice
  within their errors of the start.
- Limits. The payload of F is about 4.0 MB of the 4,194,304 bytes allowed. The fits cap the change of a row or
  column at half its norm; the global relative change of F is under 3%.

## 5. What does not help (practice data and generated puzzles, measured on the starts of section 1; do not repeat)

| tried | effect |
|---|---|
| The puzzle fit with no control | word problems 76% -> 53%, math 65% -> 54%: the short style takes over everywhere |
| Likelihood anchors in place of hold examples (900 to 2,200 of the model's own answers, also with weight 3) | the divergence on other tasks is the same as with no control (110 to 130 nats per answer); other tasks one to three points lower; the request layer keeps a half to three quarters of its gain |
| Pairs "the model's own long answer preferred to the short one" for the other tasks | no better than anchors |
| The puzzle layer at a part of its fitted change (0.25 to 0.75) | below the start on the puzzles: the layout is taken whole or not at all |
| A working for the kinds that come in many forms | gains on generated puzzles of the forms the generator makes, losses where a real instance has another form |
| The request layer fitted on top of the puzzle layer instead | the same puzzles, the requests lower (the later fit is the one that is not held) |
| The puzzle layer with 300 or 450 neurons to make room | the cost on word problems grows as the neurons shrink (-2.6 at 300) |
| A thinking opening forced by a short instruction in the prompt, or openers fitted alone | schedules up, word problems down; a third of the working's gain at best |
| Hold weight 0.05 and more | the same divergence, fewer puzzles solved |
| The request layer without hold examples at a dose above 0.35 | as published: nothing more on the requests that are not repeats, more multiple-choice answers without a readable letter |

- **One reading is one draw.** A base read four times on 400 word problems gave 75.3, 76.2, 77.6 and 76.6, then
  74.4 and 75.7: count answers, not draws, before believing a gain or a loss of one point.
- **A likelihood on sampled tokens does not see what did not happen.** What leaks from a strong fit into other
  tasks is a small change at every position; only a comparison of whole distributions, summed over the answer,
  weighs it.

## 6. Rules kept

Practice sets, generated puzzles and the models' own generations only. The requests are recombined from the
shipped practice requests and problems; no instruction is written or generated. The puzzles are made by
`derive/puzzles.py` from names, numbers and word lists in that file, checked against the question fingerprints the
toolkit ships; a working is computed from its puzzle by `derive/workings.py` and kept only if it reproduces the
puzzle's answer. Training requests use bodies from outside the case's dev and holdout splits; the puzzles kept for
the reading are never among those a fit sees. The case's dev and holdout sets are not read in the run, and nothing is tuned on a reading.
No arrays are shipped.
