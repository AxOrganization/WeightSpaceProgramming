# Separate persistent reasoning loops from successful repeated checks

A model can legitimately repeat a calculation to verify it and then continue. A persistent loop repeats the same reasoning without progressing. The hypothesis is that the two behaviors share a repetition signal and that persistence contributes an additional component. Suppress that additional component while protecting the state changes used for successful verification.

Start from the published Qwen3.5-2B Thinking champion resolved for this task at kickoff. Use the task's single start marked `parent` and its bundled `<SID>/edit/` chain. The toolkit applies that chain to the pinned base model before sampling. Stop if the task has no matching published champion. Use the toolkit's math and word-problem practice pools for fitting and validation. Use separate practice instruction and choice questions to check preservation. Deduplicate by normalized question and split by question before generating anything. Reserve a final holdout and do not use it to choose the edit. Keep all generated traces and edit arrays in the run's writable working directory.

Use up to 128 fit questions and 64 calibration questions, balanced across the two math sources. Use a final holdout of 64 math questions plus 32 questions each for instructions and choice. Cap initial and final outputs at 16,384 tokens. Resample no more than 48 fit prefixes and 24 calibration prefixes, with four continuations per prefix and a 4,096-token continuation cap. Try strength 0.5 on calibration first and try 1.0 if the smaller strength does not pass. Use two draws on the final holdout. Leave enough time for numerical checking and export, and stop if the task cannot finish within the deadline.

## Collect natural contrasts

1. Sample four completions per question from the champion on the fit and calibration pools. Require a toolkit-classified loop cutoff whose thinking marker has not closed. Tokenize the thinking and find the shortest 8–64 token motif repeated at least three times contiguously at the tail, allowing a truncated final motif. Exclude whitespace-only motifs and motifs consisting solely of prompt copies, headings, or answer formatting. End the shared prefix after two copies and before the third in that run.
2. Use `wsp.gen.generate` with the item prefix set to that still-open thinking, so the branch resumes at the selected position. Keep one outcome per question that finishes correctly, leaves the motif, and continues reasoning before closing its thinking. Compare its first 64 continuation tokens with the same 64-token window in the actual loop. Reject pairs whose fully tokenized branches do not preserve identical prefix token IDs and exactly 64 measured reasoning tokens in both windows.
3. Use other successfully finished traces as verification controls. Look for two occurrences of a calculation or check within a trace, followed by a new intermediate expression. Pair the 64-token window starting at its second occurrence with a 64-token window of progressing reasoning in the same trace. Keep pairs only when both windows precede the closing thinking marker. Match controls to loop pairs by task source and relative position in the thinking. Keep fit and calibration controls on distinct questions.
4. Require at least 12 distinct mixed-outcome fit questions, 8 separate calibration pairs, 12 fit controls and 8 calibration controls. Stop the method if the evidence does not support those comparisons. Do not construct a negative by duplicating text.

## Derive the edit

Read the matched windows with the toolkit probe. For each non-final layer, take the residual difference from looping continuation minus successful exit, and from repeated checking minus progress for the controls. Estimate the common checking direction from the mean fit control difference. Fit the loop-persistence direction by subtracting the projection on that checking direction from the mean fit loop difference and normalizing the remainder. Use the same subtraction on both fit halves. Keep layers only if the two fit-half directions have cosine at least 0.25 and at least two thirds of the calibration loop differences have positive signed projection. On the calibration verification controls, require a smaller mean absolute projection than the unprotected direction and at most half the mean absolute projection on calibration loop differences.

Estimate writer contributions by multiplying each unit's matched MLP activation difference by its down-projection alignment with this direction. Subtract the mean absolute verification-control contribution from the natural loop contribution. Rank remaining positive contributors. Discard layers where the top 64 columns do not explain at least half of that positive total. Otherwise choose the smallest set that covers half. Choose the eligible layer with highest signed agreement on calibration pairs, breaking ties by the smaller mean absolute control projection. Build a single outgoing direction ablation stacked on the champion. Select the smaller strength that reduces loop cutoffs and preserves calibration correctness within two paired standard errors. Stop if neither strength satisfies those checks. In the two-draw calibration and final holdout checks, compare against the unmodified champion with matched seeds and output budgets. Compute paired accuracy and its standard error across original questions, averaging draws within each question.

## Check and deliver

On the untouched holdout, measure paired correctness, persistent-loop cutoffs, other cutoffs, and the share of previously correct long responses that remain correct. Include ordinary check-and-continue responses, choice questions and instruction-following practice. Fix the verification subgroup from the champion's correct, finished answers containing a successful repeated check, and require at least eight distinct questions before using that subgroup. Proceed only when the persistent-loop cutoff share falls and the paired correctness result does not fall beyond two paired standard errors in any of those practice groups. Compare the effect on repeated-but-successful reasoning with the effect on persistent loops. For a separate ablation control, freeze the layer, columns and strength from calibration and replace only the protected direction with the normalized mean natural loop-minus-exit direction. Compare the two edits on the same verification prompts and the same seeds, without using the holdout to choose the submitted edit. Report the resolved champion SID, the number of distinct questions, the selected layer and strength, and the result of that protection comparison. Use the task's native numerical admission check and deliver the new edit relative to the champion SID within the official task deadline.

The toolkit's 2B loop-removal case `L15L19_k64_a6`, by AxOrganization, is the source of the directional ablation primitive and the original loop-removal observation. This extension derives contrasts from naturally observed, shared-prefix outcomes and protects the direction used during successful repeated checks. Its hypothesis is evaluated within the task.
